[size=5][b]Oblivion Frame Fixes[/b][/size]

Two small fixes that make every frame of Oblivion a little smoother, in one xOBSE plugin. Works flat and in VR.

[size=4][b]What it fixes[/b][/size]
[list=1]
[*][b]A precise frame clock.[/b] Oblivion measures how long a frame took with GetTickCount, in whole milliseconds. A 60 Hz frame of 16.67 ms reads as 16 or 17, a 144 Hz frame of 6.94 ms as 6 or 7 - so every animation and everything that moves speeds up and slows down by that much, frame by frame. On Windows the tick itself can jump in 16 ms steps when nothing has raised the timer resolution (the "64 Hz bug" that Stutter Remover and Display Tweaks fix in their way). This plugin gives the frame its real length from the high resolution counter, right inside the game's own clock code.
[*][b]A faster vertex copy.[/b] Every person in view is copied into the game's vertex buffers every frame, one memcpy call per vertex, 8 or 12 bytes each. With a few people around that was a fifth of the time a frame takes to draw. The plugin replaces that copy with one that does the same thing without the call per vertex. Same bytes, same places; only the clock can tell the difference.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Requirements[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][url=https://github.com/llde/xOBSE]xOBSE[/url]
[*]Oblivion 1.2.0.416 (Steam, GOG, or retail patched to 1.2.0.416)
[/list]

[size=4][b]Install[/b][/size]
Install with your mod manager, or copy both files into [i]Data\OBSE\Plugins\[/i]:
[list]
[*]OblivionFrameFixes.dll
[*]OblivionFrameFixes.ini
[/list]
To remove it, just delete them.

If you use DXVK, add one line to your [i]dxvk.conf[/i] next to Oblivion.exe:
[code]d3d9.cachedWriteOnlyBuffers = True[/code]
The game writes its vertex buffers through a copy, and DXVK may put those buffers into video memory, where that copy runs at PCIe speed - up to a millisecond a frame with people in view on the test machine. The line keeps them in system memory.

[size=4][b]Settings[/b][/size]
In [i]OblivionFrameFixes.ini[/i]:
[code][Main]
PreciseFrameClock=1
FastStreamCopy=1

[Diagnostics]
FrameClockTrace=0
StreamCopyCheck=0[/code]
[list]
[*][b]PreciseFrameClock[/b] and [b]FastStreamCopy[/b]: 1 turns the fix on, 0 leaves that part of the game as it is.
[*][b]FrameClockTrace[/b]: 1 writes a line every ten seconds with the frame lengths the game's own clock produced and the lengths the frames were given. Good for seeing the problem on your machine; off for play.
[*][b]StreamCopyCheck[/b]: 1 does every vertex copy twice, the game's way and the fast way, and compares them byte for byte. Slow; only for checking.
[/list]
[i]OblivionFrameFixes.log[/i] in the game folder says what the plugin did.

[size=4][b]Good to know[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]Oblivion 1.2.416 only.[/b] Both fixes patch two spots in the game's code; the plugin checks the bytes there first and does nothing if they are not the game's own. The log says which.
[*][b]OBVR[/b], the VR mod, carries both fixes itself. With OBVR you don't need this plugin; if both are installed, whichever comes second finds the spot patched and steps aside. Harmless.
[*][b]Display Tweaks and Stutter Remover[/b] replace GetTickCount for the whole game. This plugin changes only the frame's length, one level below that, so they can be used together: the plugin's precise length wins for the frame, theirs still serves the game's other timers.
[*][b]Measured so far[/b] in VR under Proton with DXVK, in the people-heavy opening scene: the scene render a fifth to a quarter faster with the fast copy (3.22 ms before, 2.37 to 2.57 ms with it and the dxvk.conf line); the game's clock reading 13 or 14 ms at 75 Hz before the fix and 13.33 after; and with StreamCopyCheck=1 522,000 vertex copies compared with the game's own, 0 differences. Flat numbers and the Windows tick are still to be measured; FrameClockTrace=1 shows the second one on your machine.
[/list]
